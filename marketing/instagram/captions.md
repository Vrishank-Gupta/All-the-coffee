## Post 3: AI hallucination, explained

Image: `project/Hallucination.dc.html` · Blog: none

```
"The contract was signed on 14 March 2024, for ₹4.2 lakh."

Specific date. Specific amount. Said with complete confidence. And entirely made up.

That's an AI hallucination: the tool stating something false as if it were certain. It isn't lying, and it isn't broken. Chat tools generate the most likely-sounding next words, and a confident, specific answer often sounds more likely than "I don't know."

This happens to serious people, not just beginners. In December 2024, a tax tribunal bench in Bengaluru withdrew one of its own orders after it turned out to cite court judgments that don't exist, reportedly pulled from a chatbot without anyone checking.

The fix isn't to stop using AI. It's a habit:
→ Treat any name, date, number, quote or legal reference from AI as a draft, not a fact.
→ Check it against the source document before it goes into an email, a report or a presentation.
→ When you can, give the AI the document itself and ask it to answer only from that.

Checking AI output is one of the first things our free course teaches, because it's what makes everything you build with AI safe to rely on. Link in bio.

## Post 4: What is an API?

Image: `project/Api.dc.html` · Blog: none

```
Someone in the meeting says "we'll just call their API." Everyone nods. You nod too, and make a mental note to Google it later.

Here's the restaurant version.

You order pasta. You never walk into the kitchen. You don't know who's cooking or how. You tell the waiter, the waiter takes the order in, and your food comes out.

An API is that waiter, sitting between two pieces of software. One side asks for something in an agreed format, the other side does the work and sends back an answer. Neither has to know how the other works inside.

You use this every day. When you pay with a UPI app, the app doesn't reach into your bank directly. It sends a request through UPI, the system run by NPCI, which asks your bank, and the answer comes back in a couple of seconds. Waiters all the way down.

That's the whole word. Next time someone says "API" in a meeting, you'll know exactly what they mean.

Later in our course you'll put AI inside a tool you built yourself, and this is the idea that makes that possible. The first module is free: link in bio.

## Post 14: Legal, fake citations reach the Supreme Court

Image: `project/PostLegalCitations.dc.html` · Blog: none

```
In July 2026, the Supreme Court set aside orders of the NCLT and the NCLAT in an insolvency matter. The reason: the judgments those orders relied on didn't exist. They were, in all likelihood, AI-generated, and nobody caught it until the appeal reached the Supreme Court.

This isn't a reason to stay away from AI. The Court itself said its ruling wasn't meant to stop the legitimate use of AI. It's a reason to be very precise about which part you hand over.

Where AI earns its keep in legal work:
→ A first-pass summary of a long agreement, so you know where to look
→ Checking a draft against your own checklist and flagging what's missing
→ A one-page issue list to start your review from, not to end it

What you never hand over:
→ Whether a case, section or clause it mentions actually exists and says what it claims. That means opening the original, every time, before it goes into a draft, a notice or an opinion.

A practical habit: when you ask AI about a contract, tell it to give the clause number for every point and to cite no case law at all. Then you can check every line against the document in minutes.

And only use the AI tools your firm has approved for client documents.

## Post 15: HR, the old spreadsheet and DPDP

Image: `project/PostHrSpreadsheet.dc.html` · Blog: `/blog/dpdp-employee-data-hr`

```
Somewhere on your shared drive there's a file called something like "Emp_Master_FINAL_v3_2019.xlsx". It has names, phone numbers, PANs, maybe bank details. Half the people in it left years ago. Nobody remembers who has access.

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act phases in, and most of the duties that matter to HR apply from 13 May 2027. In plain terms: keep personal data secure, keep it only as long as you genuinely need it, and report a breach when one happens. Employment is a recognised reason to process employee data, but that's a reason you're allowed to use it, not an exemption from keeping it safe.

Two habits that help right now, long before any deadline:

→ Before you paste anything into an AI tool, swap names, PANs and phone numbers for codes like EMP-014. AI doesn't need to know who someone is to fix your formula or rewrite your letter.
→ Make a simple list of where employee data actually lives: Excel sheets, Google Forms responses, WhatsApp groups, inboxes, the shared drive. You can't tidy what you can't find.

Five-minute start: search your shared drive for "PAN" and see what comes up. That's where your clean-up begins.
